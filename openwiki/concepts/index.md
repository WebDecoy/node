# Files

- [Bot Classification, Robots Policy, and Web Bot Auth](bot-identity-policy-and-web-bot-auth.md) - Separate self-declared User-Agent classification and robots.txt policy from cryptographic Web Bot Auth verification. Use the former for cooperative crawler policy and the latter to identify trusted signed agents or known-agent impersonation without adding network work to warm requests.
- [Self-Hosted Captcha and Browser-Signal Decision Flow](captcha-and-browser-signals.md) - How WebDecoy's browser widget, proof-of-work, in-process detection, signed captcha tokens, and client-signal rule fit together. Includes the persistence and integration boundaries required to make a browser score affect later requests safely.
- [Client Identity, Proxy Trust, Characteristics, and Edge Context](client-identity-proxy-trust-and-edge-context.md) - How WebDecoy derives the client address and reusable caller key, and how proxy topology changes the safety of enforcement and reporting. It also explains advisory edge verdict headers, their framework entry points, and the origin-isolation and caching conditions for using them safely.
- [Request Protection, Decisions, and Enforcement Semantics](request-decisions-and-enforcement.md) - How WebDecoy turns normalized request metadata into local rule outcomes, optional remote detection, typed decisions, and framework-specific enforcement responses. Covers cache boundaries, monitor mode, fail-open errors, and the operational signals needed to roll out protection safely.
- [Rules, Shared State, Honeytokens, and Attack-Signature Boundaries](rules-rate-limits-and-deception.md) - How WebDecoy evaluates ordered local rules, records outcomes and violations, shares rate-limit state, and applies tripwire, honeytoken, bot, filter, and attack-signature controls. Explains dry runs, async prerequisites, operational failure policies, and the deliberately narrow boundary of payload signatures.
